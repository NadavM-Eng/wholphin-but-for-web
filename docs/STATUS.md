## 1. Does it work?

Yes, as far as automated checks and agents in headless browsers can tell, on `main`, and in the owner's first test on a touch device. Every feature of the TV app that the project planned to port is built and wired into one app: sign-in, the shell, Home and its customization, library browsing, detail pages, search, playback with the on-screen player, settings, Seerr (Discover), Live TV, music with a mini player, photos with a slideshow, the screensaver, and the production server with its Docker image. The TV-comparison pass compared every reference picture of the TV app with the web app and is part of `main`. Thirteen independent checkers then looked at the merged app from thirteen angles and reported 47 findings (6 major, 41 minor); all 47 are closed, and an independent audit returned "ready to land". A polish round then closed most of what that check had left open (merge notes section 13), and its independent verifier also returned "ready to land". A closing round then fixed how values such as ".25" or "100 Mbps" are drawn in Hebrew and Arabic and found the causes of three open observations, and a touch fix made a finger that starts on a slider scroll the page (section 2, steps 10 and 12). On `main` (`7ad52ba`) the lead measured: typecheck clean, 776 unit tests in 87 files pass, lint without errors, the production build passes, and the end-to-end suite, now 250 tests in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit, passed 250 of 250 (section 4.1). On the polish round's final tree the screenshot crawler took 390 pictures in English and 390 in Hebrew, all ok and without warnings, the TV compare sheet's 208 scenes ran without errors, and the dynamic backdrop colours of all 11 backdrops that show colours in the TV reference pictures match the TV within 1/255. Since 2026-11-18 the app also runs as a trial behind a reverse proxy over HTTPS, in a container built from `7ad52ba`: the lead's smoke test through the proxy passed (section 4.1), and the owner opened it on their phone and reported that everything works. The owner named one touch problem, a finger that lands on a settings slider while scrolling changed the slider at once; it is fixed in `7ad52ba`. The owner also set the order of work: the desktop browser version must be flawless first, phone and touch interface problems come after it ([DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md) O-28). What is not known: how it behaves in real Safari, in the oldest supported Chrome and Firefox versions, on an HDR screen, with a real microphone and with real Seerr requests, because none of those were available to the agents; and on real phones and tablets beyond the owner's one test, whose pages and browser are not recorded.

8. **Backdrop colours** (branch `colors`): the dynamic backdrop colours differed from the TV on some items. The proven cause: before picking colours, Android's palette library shrinks the image with `Bitmap.createScaledBitmap(bitmap, w, h, false)`, which goes through Skia's fixed-point nearest-neighbour sampling; the web used plain `floor((d + 0.5) * src / dst)` and, for a 1920x1080 backdrop, took a neighbouring pixel in 16 of 150 columns and 12 of 84 rows, enough to change the picked colours. `src/app/shell/extractColors.ts` now reproduces Skia's sampling, and Now Playing takes its colours from the stored image. The JPEG-decoder explanation recorded by the comparison pass was refuted: the 16 test backdrops decode byte-identically in all four browser engines and in libjpeg-turbo. Home rows 4 and 5 of the compare sheet were not a colour defect (albums have no backdrop, both apps keep the previous item's colours; the compare scene walked the rows too fast, fixed in `tools/shots/tvScenes.ts`). Result: 11 of 11 backdrops that show colours in the reference pictures match the TV within 1/255 (6 of 11 before); an independent verifier measured 20 reference pictures with its own script and found no regression.
9. **Polish round** (merge notes section 13, branch `polish`): six fixers worked side by side on files that did not overlap (shell focus; grids; the series overview's scroll; stylesheets for the oldest supported engines; tools and translations; the Live TV player overlay), one integrator read and committed their work, and an independent verifier checked it. The integrator found one regression of the round and repaired it (the page moved by itself when a mouse user came Back to a "View more" grid). Several items turned out to be upstream behaviour, not reproducible, or server data (merge notes 13.1). Ten new end-to-end tests. Verdict of the verifier: "ready to land", no blocking finding. The lead merged it (`f1fb308`), reset the test server, corrected one test that depended on the shared server's data (`c358a52`) and pushed.
10. **Closing round** (merge notes section 14, commit `692715f`): one workflow with three documentation writers, one agent that retook the nine README screenshots, one small-fix agent and one independent checker. The small-fix agent took the four points the polish round's verifier and notes had left. Fixed: in Hebrew and Arabic, values made of digits, signs and Latin units were drawn out of order (".25" as "25.", "-1.5s" as "1.5s-", "100 Mbps" as "Mbps 100") in the player's sheets, the subtitle delay dialog and its step buttons, the settings slider summaries, the two shortcut toasts and the debug overlay; English is unchanged.
