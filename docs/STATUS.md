## 1. Does it work?

Yes, as far as automated checks and agents in headless browsers can tell, on `main`, in the owner's first test on a touch device, and in the owner's looks at the trial since. The rest of this paragraph is the state at the end of the build (`7ad52ba`, 2026-11-18); the next paragraph says what has changed up to `509db33`. Every feature of the TV app that the project planned to port is built and wired into one app: sign-in, the shell, Home and its customization, library browsing, detail pages, search, playback with the on-screen player, settings, Seerr (Discover), Live TV, music with a mini player, photos with a slideshow, the screensaver, and the production server with its Docker image. The TV-comparison pass compared every reference picture of the TV app with the web app and is part of `main`. Thirteen independent checkers then looked at the merged app from thirteen angles and reported 47 findings (6 major, 41 minor); all 47 are closed, and an independent audit returned "ready to land". A polish round then closed most of what that check had left open (merge notes section 13), and its independent verifier also returned "ready to land". A closing round then fixed how values such as ".25" or "100 Mbps" are drawn in Hebrew and Arabic and found the causes of three open observations, and a touch fix made a finger that starts on a slider scroll the page (section 2, steps 10 and 12). On `main` at `7ad52ba` the lead measured: typecheck clean, 776 unit tests in 87 files pass, lint without errors, the production build passes, and the end-to-end suite, then 250 tests in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit, passed 250 of 250 (section 4.1). On the polish round's final tree the screenshot crawler took 390 pictures in English and 390 in Hebrew, all ok and without warnings, the TV compare sheet's 208 scenes ran without errors, and the dynamic backdrop colours of all 11 backdrops that show colours in the TV reference pictures match the TV within 1/255. Since 2026-11-18 the app also runs as a trial behind a reverse proxy over HTTPS, at first in a container built from `7ad52ba`: the lead's smoke test through the proxy passed (section 4.1), and the owner opened it on their phone and reported that everything works. The owner named one touch problem, a finger that lands on a settings slider while scrolling changed the slider at once; it is fixed in `7ad52ba`. The owner also set the order of work: the desktop browser version must be flawless first, phone and touch interface problems come after it ([DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md) O-28). What is not known: how it behaves in real Safari, in the oldest supported Chrome and Firefox versions, on an HDR screen, with a real microphone and with real Seerr requests, because none of those were available to the agents; and on real phones and tablets beyond the owner's one test, whose pages and browser are not recorded.

Since then the project was published and went into maintenance (section 2, steps 13 to 17; [PROJECT-LOG.md](PROJECT-LOG.md) phases 31 to 34). Before the release the interface scale stopped resizing the page while its slider is dragged, the audio-track button became a music note ([DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md) O-32), the documents lost everything that describes one machine, network or server (O-33), and the player changed in the two ways the owner had named as must-haves (O-34): a compact control strip that does not darken the picture, and far less converting on the server, because desktop Chromium now plays Matroska files, PGS subtitles and HDR10 itself (section 3.1). The repository went public on 2026-11-18 as a clean copy (O-35, T-58). The maintenance of 2026-11-22 to 24 took four reported bugs (three fixed in the app: a poster grid that shook sideways when it just fitted, a slider whose mouse drag the browser took over, subtitle lines that took the interface's direction; for the fourth, the video box was measured, found to be exactly the window already, and pinned with a test), reworked the player's strips twice more, removed the Back button where the drawer is beside the page, made the open drawer lie over the page, turned Settings into a panel over the page it was opened from, signs a listed user without a password in by the click, gives the overview in the Home header more room, and put the subtitle style options into the player's settings. On `main` at `509db33` the lead measured: the type check clean, 897 unit tests in 101 files pass, no new lint warning in the changed files; the browser tests of each changed area passed when the change was made. What is weaker than at the end of the build: the whole end-to-end suite was not run after each change but once, over phases 32 to 34, before the second release (phase 35; 336 tests, two faults of tests found and repaired), because small changes now take a fast path with the slow checks after it (T-63). The owner looked at each change on the trial and answered in words; the lead looked at headless pictures and, for animated changes, at recorded frames (T-62), never at a visible window, and for the builds of phases 33 and 34 not at the live trial. The second public release of 2026-11-24 carries all of it into the public repository.

### 3.1 Playback: what the browser plays by itself, and what the server still does

The owner's first must-have before shipping was less converting on the server ([DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md) O-34; how it was done is T-59). The table is a summary of the last part of the playback matrix (`src/features/playback/engine/PLAYBACK_MATRIX.md`, "Playing without the server converting", 2026-11-18): the app's device profile was built inside each headless browser and the throwaway test server was asked for its playback answer for every test file, once per audio track and once per kind of subtitle, before and after `bf31148`. "Direct" means the file itself is sent and nothing runs on the server. "Repackaged" means the server puts the streams into another container and copies them. "Audio converted" means the video is copied and only the sound is encoded again. "Re-encoded" means the picture itself is encoded again, the expensive case.

| Source | Edge and Chrome (desktop Chromium) | Firefox (Playwright's build) | WebKit (Playwright's build on Windows) |
|---|---|---|---|
| MP4 with H.264 and AAC; WebM with AV1 or VP9 | direct, as before | direct, as before | direct, as before |
| Matroska with H.264 or HEVC and AAC | repackaged before, **direct** now | repackaged before, **direct** now by the server's answer; this build cannot decode HEVC: the HEVC file with E-AC-3 sound that was run through the app in it fell down the ladder to a re-encode, and the one with AAC was not run there | repackaged, as before (H.264; an HEVC picture is re-encoded there, see below) |
| Matroska with AC-3 or E-AC-3 sound | audio converted, before and after (the reason is now the audio codec alone, no longer the container too) | the same | repackaged before, audio converted now |
| HDR10 on a display that is not HDR | re-encoded before, **direct** now (the browser converts it for the display) | re-encoded, as before | re-encoded, as before |
| Matroska with a text or ASS subtitle chosen | repackaged before, **direct** now; the subtitle is drawn in the browser both times | the same | repackaged, as before |
| A PGS subtitle chosen | re-encoded with the subtitle burned in before, **direct** now, drawn in the browser | the same | re-encoded with the subtitle burned in before; by the server's answer now repackaged, with the subtitle for the browser (not played) |
| A VobSub subtitle chosen | re-encoded with the subtitle burned in, as before | the same | the same |
| The second audio track of a file | repackaged, video and audio copied; the first track: **direct** | the same | repackaged |
| MPEG-2; MPEG-4 ASP in AVI | re-encoded, as before | the same | the same |

Edge and Chrome gave the same answer on every row. The WebKit column is taken from the server's reasons alone: for a source with HEVC video the picture is re-encoded there whatever the row says, because that profile offers only H.264 in HLS (the matrix carries the same note). Through the app, with the server's own report, the matrix records in Edge: direct play of H.264 and of 4K HEVC in Matroska, of HDR10 with the colours matching the server's tone-mapped version of the same frame, PGS and ASS drawn over a directly played file, and an AC-3 5.1 track played with the video copied and the audio converted to AAC with six channels. None of the browsers that were tested decodes AC-3 or E-AC-3 (Edge would need a decoder from the operating system), so that row is what such a browser does; Safari was not available. An audio-only conversion cost about 1.4 CPU-seconds per minute of film on the throwaway server, against about 12 for re-encoding even an easy 720p picture. Playback answers read on the real server, read-only and without streaming anything, are in the matrix as well. Not measured after `bf31148` by an agent: any of this with real films played on a real server. The Debug page's section "Measured Playback Support" shows what a given browser does.
